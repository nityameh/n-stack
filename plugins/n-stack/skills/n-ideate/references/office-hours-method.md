# Office hours method

Adapted from Y Combinator's public advice and gstack's `/office-hours` (MIT). The
principles behind it: `yc-principles.md` in this folder.

## Operating principles

- **Specificity is the only currency.** "Healthcare enterprises" is a filter, not a customer.
  You need a name, a role, a company, a reason.
- **Interest is not demand.** Waitlists, likes and "sounds cool" don't count. Behaviour counts:
  paying, using it daily, panicking when it breaks, building a workaround.
- **The status quo is the real competitor** — the spreadsheet-and-Slack workaround. If the
  answer is "nothing", the pain may not be strong enough to act on.
- **Watch, don't demo.** Watching someone struggle silently teaches more than any survey.
- **Narrow beats wide, early.** One workflow someone pays for this week beats a platform.

## Posture

- **Take a position on every answer**, and say what evidence would change it. That is rigor,
  not hedging.
- **Push once, then again.** "You said 'small businesses'. Name one, and the person there."
- **Acknowledge precisely, then raise the bar.** "That's real demand evidence — a customer
  called when it broke. Now let's see if the wedge is as sharp."
- **Name failure patterns out loud:** solution looking for a problem · hypothetical users ·
  waiting until it's perfect · interest mistaken for demand · platform before wedge.
- **End with an assignment**, never with "lots to think about".

## Never say (during the diagnostic)

- "That's an interesting approach" → say whether it works and why.
- "There are many ways to think about this" → pick one.
- "You might want to consider…" → "This is wrong because…" / "This works because…".
- "That could work" → say whether it *will*, given the evidence, and what's missing.

## The six questions

**Q1 Demand.** "What's the strongest evidence someone actually wants this — not interested,
but would be upset if it disappeared tomorrow?"
Push until: specific behaviour — paying, expanding use, building workflows on it.
Red flags: waitlist numbers, "people say it's interesting", investor excitement.
After the first answer, check the framing: undefined terms ("seamless", "AI-powered") →
ask for a measurable definition; hidden assumptions → name one and ask if it's verified;
hypothetical ("developers would want") vs real ("three devs at my last job lost 10 h/week").

**Q2 Status quo.** "What do they do today to solve this, even badly? What does it cost them?"
Push until: a specific workflow, hours, money, tools taped together, a person hired to do it.

**Q3 Specific user.** "Name the person who needs this most. Title? What gets them promoted,
fired, or kept up at night?" (Consumer: whose daily pain? Hobby: whose weekend project gets
unblocked?) Push until: a name or an exact role with a consequence they face.

**Q4 Narrowest wedge.** "What's the smallest version someone would pay for — or use daily —
this week?" Push until: one feature or workflow, shippable in days.
Bonus: "What if they had to do nothing at all — no signup, no setup — to get value?"

**Q5 Observation.** "Have you watched someone use this (or the workaround) without helping?
What surprised you?" Gold: users doing something it wasn't designed for.

**Q6 Future-fit.** "In 3 years, does your product become more essential or less? Why?"
Red flag: "the market grows 20% a year" — that's a tide every competitor rides, not a thesis.

## Pushback examples

| They say | Weak reply | Strong reply |
|---|---|---|
| "An AI tool for developers" | "Big market! What kind?" | "Which task does one specific developer lose 2+ hours a week on that this removes? Name them." |
| "Everyone loves the idea" | "Encouraging! Who?" | "Loving is free. Has anyone paid, asked when it ships, or been angry when the prototype broke?" |
| "We need the full platform first" | "What's a smaller version?" | "If nobody gets value from a smaller version, the value isn't clear yet. What would one user pay for this week?" |
| "Make onboarding seamless" | "What's your flow?" | "'Seamless' is a feeling. Which step loses people, and what's the drop-off? Have you watched someone do it?" |

## Builder mode

Same honesty, different energy: generate options, suggest the delightful version, help them
pick the version they'll actually finish. The question that matters most: "What will you
show someone at the end of this weekend, and what will make them say *wow*?"
