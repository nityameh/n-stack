# YC principles for ideation

Paraphrased from Y Combinator's public essays and talks (Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston,
Michael Seibel, the YC Startup School library). Read the originals: paulgraham.com/articles.html,
ycombinator.com/library.

## Core

- **Make something people want.** Everything else is secondary. Evidence beats opinion.
- **Talk to users.** Before building, while building, after launching. Watch what they do,
  not what they say they would do.
- **Start with a small, intense need.** Better 100 people who love it than 10,000 who think
  it's nice. "Well vs. wide": go deep on a few users first.
- **Do things that don't scale.** Recruit users by hand, onboard them personally, do the
  work manually behind the scenes until you know what to automate.
- **Launch early, iterate fast.** If you're not embarrassed by v1 you launched too late.
  Weekly cycles: build → ship → talk to users → measure.
- **Pick one metric.** Usually weekly active users or revenue. Grow it 5-7% a week.
- **Default alive.** Know whether you reach profitability on current growth before the
  money runs out.
- **Live in the future, build what's missing.** The best ideas come from noticing what's
  missing in your own work and life, especially at the edge of a new technology.
- **Founder-market fit.** Why are *you* the person to build this? Domain insight, access to
  users, or obsession.

## Good-idea signals

- You have the problem yourself, or have watched people suffer it closely.
- People already pay for a bad solution, or hack together their own.
- A recent change (new tech, regulation, platform, behaviour) makes a new solution possible.
- The wedge is small enough to build in weeks and valuable enough that users would be upset
  to lose it.
- It sounds a bit unimpressive or "too small" — but has a path to large.

## Red flags

- **Tarpit idea:** sounds great, many have tried, nobody succeeded, and there is a structural
  reason (e.g. "an app to help friends pick a restaurant", "a social network for X",
  "a better to-do list"). Ask: why did the previous attempts fail, and what's different now?
- **Solution looking for a problem:** starts from a technology ("use AI for…") rather than
  a user's pain.
- **No specific user:** "everyone", "businesses", "developers".
- **Evidence is only compliments:** "people said they'd use it". Compliments are not demand.
- **Schlep blindness:** avoiding a great idea because it involves boring, painful work
  (payments, compliance, sales). The schlep is often the moat.
- **Too broad a v1:** a platform, a marketplace with both sides empty, a super-app.
- **Distribution is "it'll go viral".** Name the first 10 users by channel.

## Questions to keep asking

- Who is the user, exactly? What were they doing right before they needed this?
- How do they solve it today, and how much do they hate it?
- What's the smallest version they'd pay for, or use daily?
- What do you believe that most people disagree with?
- If this works, why doesn't an incumbent copy it in a month?
